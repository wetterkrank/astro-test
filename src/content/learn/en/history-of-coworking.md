Coworking did not start as a product category. It grew out of people who needed desks, community, and a lighter way to run a business than a traditional office lease.

## Early roots

Spaces like C-Base in Berlin and early San Francisco experiments showed that shared infrastructure could support independent work. Brad Neuberg’s 2005 coworking effort gave the model a name that stuck.

### [St. Oberholz and European growth](#)

European cafés and early hubs proved demand for day passes, community events, and flexible memberships — the same building blocks Cobot was built to manage.

## Why history still matters for operators

- Members expect community, not just desks.
- Admin grows faster than headcount unless billing and access are automated.
- Local values and niche focus still differentiate spaces.
